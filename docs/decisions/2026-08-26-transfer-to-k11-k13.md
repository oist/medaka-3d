# The uncapped arm on K_1_1 and K_1_3, without choosing a lambda

**Date:** 2026-08-26. **Written before the run.** Only "Result" is appended after.
**Status:** measurement only. Nothing is promoted. `c03` still owns the fixed
output paths. **No existing tool is modified and no existing output is
overwritten** — `c24` and `c25` are imported by the new tools, never edited.
**Follows:** [2026-08-26 stage A vs correspondence](2026-08-26-stage-a-vs-correspondence.md),
which found the gain is carried by the uncapping, and
[2026-08-26 the lambda selector](2026-08-26-lambda-selector.md), which found
`kappa = 0.2371` is not an oracle and that the pre-registered rule does not
identify `lambda` on this data.
**Tools:** new `c27` (stage A uncapped, any experiment), new `c28` (the
correspondence across the whole `lambda` grid, and the transfer report).

## Why there is no lambda in this document

The 2026-08-18 rule selects `lambda` by held-out node cross-validation. The audit
of 2026-08-26 showed that on K_1_2 it resolves a factor of 3.16 in `lambda` at
0.09–0.41% of its own score, with one direction of one run a tie at 0.002%, and
that the two runs said to have agreed on `kappa = 0.2371` in fact chose grid
points 3.16x apart in each direction — only the geometric mean coincided, and
only because the two grid indices happened to sum to the same number.

So **no point `lambda` is chosen here, and none is reported as chosen.** The
whole grid is run and a claim is carried forward only if it holds at every point
of it. This is the falsifiability C needs and the selector cannot supply: on
K_1_1 and K_1_3 there is no `c09`, so a `lambda` picked after the fact could
never be checked — but a statement true across the range needs no pick.

**The grid is inherited, not selected:** `lambda` ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.767, 2,
8} mm, exactly the seven arms already stored for K_1_2 on 2026-08-25. Taking
K_1_2's own set is what makes the columns comparable, and it removes the choice.

## What this can and cannot do

**Cannot score identity.** `female_sex_gt.csv` covers K_1_2 alone. There is no
`c09` on K_1_1 or K_1_3, no preserved/break/swap, and **no claim about identity
error will be made from this run.**

**Can test transfer** of everything the reconstruction reports about itself, and
that is the entire purpose.

**The caveat travels with every number below.** K_1_1, K_1_2 and K_1_3 are the
**same three fish on three consecutive days**. Agreement across them is repeated
measures, not replication. `n_groups = 1`. Generalisation means a different
group, and 23 of the 24 trials have never been analysed.

A K_1_1 or K_1_3 value is reported as sitting with one population, with the

## What will be read, and how — decided now

For each of K_1_1 and K_1_3, at **every** `lambda` in the grid:

1. **Stage A** — labels per camera, boundary renames, instances left unlabelled.
2. **The state space** — mean and max states per frame.
3. **The reconstruction** — `resolved`, three cameras on every animal.
4. **The residual tail** — median, p99, p99.9, count over 4 mm, over three-camera
   animal-frames, computed exactly as the reference column was.
5. **Slot label changes**, split 1 / 2 / 3 slots.
6. **The gate `derive_gate` would have set**, recorded but **not used** — the
   linker's gate is held at `c19`'s 4.0 mm / 73 frames, which is post-hoc, as it
   has been for every arm since `c21`. A derived gate is a property of its own
   reconstruction and is not comparable across two.

**The reading rules, fixed before the numbers exist:**

* A quantity **transfers** when the K_1_1 and K_1_3 values lie inside the
  interval K_1_2 spans across the same grid. Where one does not, the gap is
  reported in that quantity's own unit and the quantity is called
  **day-dependent** — not better, not worse.
* A claim is **carried forward only if it holds at every `lambda` in the grid.**
  A claim true at some `lambda` and false at another is recorded as
  `lambda`-dependent and carried no further. This is the point of running the
  grid rather than a point.
* **`0` unlabelled instances is a binary check**, not a comparison. If stage A
  leaves any instance unlabelled on either day, that is a failure of the
  uncapped arm and is reported as one.
* **No day is ranked against another**, and no aggregate across the three days
  is formed. They are one trio of fish on three consecutive days; an average
  over them would state a precision that does not exist.

## What is deliberately not in this run

* **The capped arm as a per-day control.** Reproducing the 20x tail collapse on
  K_1_1 and K_1_3 would need `c16`'s tracker on those days too. It is the single
  most valuable extension and it is **not** done here: it doubles stage A, and
  chunking a *capped* tracker is not covered by `c24`'s partition-equivalence
  check, which was measured with the cap off. Offered, not assumed.
* **`c22` review packs.** New scenes covering **males** are the only way that
  evidence grows, and they need a human at the end of them. A separate activity.
* **Any `c09` run.** There is no female annotation on these days. `c09`'s fixed
  output stays at the `c03` baseline — preserved 269 / break 19 / swap 8 — and is
  verified unchanged at the end.

## Result

**C did not start. The pre-registered chunking check failed on every experiment,
including K_1_2, and the failure retracts a claim in the 2026-08-25 record.**

### The check, run across days and views

`c24`'s `--check-chunking`, imported unmodified: track one chunk at warm-up 500
and at 2,000 and compare the **partition** of the instances in it — ids are not
comparable between runs, groupings are. **0 differing is the pass.**

| day | view | window 12,000–24,000 | window 24,000–48,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| K_1_1 | `top_cam` | **0** | — |
| K_1_1 | `side1_cam` | 12,160 / 34,978 = **34.8%** | — |
| K_1_1 | `side2_cam` | 20,064 / 35,647 = **56.3%** | — |
| K_1_2 | `top_cam` | **0** | 39,552 / 71,649 = **55.2%** |
| K_1_2 | `side1_cam` | **0** of 35,580 | 61,551 / 70,147 = **87.7%** |
| K_1_2 | `side2_cam` | 30,754 / 35,531 = **86.6%** | — |
| K_1_3 | `top_cam` | 13,374 / 35,870 = **37.3%** | — |
| K_1_3 | `side1_cam` | 21,700 / 35,379 = **61.3%** | — |
| K_1_3 | `side2_cam` | 22,356 / 35,209 = **63.5%** | — |

**Three passes in eleven measurements, and all three are the same window.**

`c27` reproduces `c24` exactly where `c24` was measured: **0 of 35,580** on
K_1_2 `side1_cam`, the instance count identifying the cell — `35,580` is
`side1_cam`, not the default `top_cam`. So the failures below are not a
difference between the two tools.

### Raising the warm-up makes it worse, and the reason is in the record

K_1_3 `top_cam`: warm-up 500 vs 2,000 differs on **37.3%**; warm-up 2,000 vs
8,000 differs on **45.9%**. It is not converging, and the 2026-08-25 decision
already says why, two sections earlier than the claim it undermines:

> `LocalQueueCandidates.current_tracks` is appended to and never emptied, so a
> track last seen 300,000 frames ago is still a candidate.

More warm-up means a larger candidate set, so the tracker's behaviour is a
function of how much history it has seen. **A chunked run is not an
approximation of a serial run that a longer warm-up converges to. It is a
different computation**, and the serial run — which carries the whole recording
in `current_tracks` — is the limit none of these chunks approach.

### What this retracts

[2026-08-25 uncapped tracklets](2026-08-25-uncapped-tracklets.md), the `c24`
section:

> `--check-chunking` runs one chunk at the stated warm-up and at four times it
> and compares the **partition** (ids are not comparable between runs): **0 of
> 35,580 instances differ**, so the parallel run is equivalent to the serial one
> away from the boundaries.

**The measurement stands; the "so" does not.** One cell — K_1_2, `side1_cam`,
frames 12,000–24,000 — was checked out of 30 chunks per view across three views,
and it is one of the three cells that pass. The same view fails at the next
boundary out (87.7%) and the third camera fails at the checked one (86.6%).

This is the same shape as the round-7/round-8 error recorded in `CLAUDE.md`: **an
assertion read as a measurement.** The difference is that here the measurement
was real and only its scope was assumed.

### What it does and does not touch

**Does not touch:** the measured properties of the stored K_1_2 reconstruction.
The residual tail did collapse 20x, `c25` did commit 0 swaps on the 298 female
intervals, 15,487 instances that had no label do have one. Those are
measurements of the labelling that exists.

**Does touch:** what that labelling *is*. It is the output of a **30-way chunked**
tracking run, not of "`c16`'s tracker with the cap removed". Every description of
`c24` as the latter is now wrong, and the run is reproducible only as the chunked
computation it actually was.

**Touches, and this is the sharp one:** [the stage A versus correspondence
result](2026-08-26-stage-a-vs-correspondence.md) of earlier today. It compared
`c16`'s labels against `c24`'s and attributed the difference to the uncapping.
`c16` ran **serially**; `c24` ran **30-way chunked**. The comparison therefore
confounds two changes, exactly as `c24` and `c25` were confounded before Step A
separated them.

And the confound has a plausible direction rather than being merely possible:
chunking **bounds** how stale a candidate track can be, because each chunk starts
with an empty `current_tracks`. Unbounded staleness is a named defect of this
tracker. So some of the 20x tail collapse may be the chunking limiting that
defect rather than the cap coming off. **How much is not known and cannot be
read off any existing output.**

### What C needs before it can run

Stage A has to be run **serially**, with no chunking, on every day including
K_1_2 — otherwise the transfer comparison compares chunk layouts as much as
days. `c24` measured the serial tracker at **56 minutes per camera**, and the
three cameras are independent, so a day costs about an hour of wall clock and
the three K days about three.

That re-run produces labels that differ from the stored `c24` output, so every
number in the 2026-08-25 record would need recomputing against it before the two
could be compared. That is a large consequence and it is **recorded here for a
decision, not acted on.**

Design rule 5 — *no flag may change which algorithm runs* — is the relevant one.
`--chunk` demonstrably changes which algorithm runs. It is not a flag on `c24`'s
method; it is a different method, and the repository's own rule says it needs a
different command or none.

### Not done, and not implied

No `c25` was run on any day. No `lambda` grid was swept. `c09` was not run at
all; its fixed output stands at the `c03` baseline — preserved 269 / break 19 /
swap 8 — and was verified unchanged. `c24`'s output directory was not written to;
`c27` writes under `c27_uncapped_tracklets_any/<experiment>/`. **No claim about
transfer to K_1_1 or K_1_3 is made in this document, because none was measured.**

### Read against the rules fixed before the run

**Transfers.**

* **The residual tail sits with the uncapped population on every day at every
  `lambda`.** p99.9 runs 3.47–8.35 mm across all 21 arms, against the capped
  arm's **72.56 mm**. Nothing lands between the two populations; the separation
  is a factor of 9 at worst and 21 at best.
* **Zero unlabelled instances**, all three days. Binary, and it passes.
* **The shape of the `lambda` dependence transfers exactly.** On every day,
