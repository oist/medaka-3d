## The finding that governs everything else

**The round-8 labels are not independent of the round-7 top-down model.**

On the held-out frames, **47.8% (top) and 48.4% (side) of ground-truth instances
are byte-identical (≤ 0.01 px) to a round-7 top-down prediction. The figure for
bottom-up is 0.0%.** The round-8 labelling pass was done by correcting top-down
output, and about half of it was accepted unchanged.

So there is **no unbiased evaluation set for round-7 top-down in the existing
labels**, and any single number comparing the architectures is biased in a known
direction. Every result below is therefore reported as a **bracket**:

| arm | what it contains | bias |
|---|---|---|
| **ALL** | every GT instance | favours **top-down** — half the "truth" is its own output |
| **CORRECTED** | only instances the human overrode | favours **bottom-up** — keeps exactly where top-down was wrong |

The true value lies between them. A conclusion is only trustworthy here if it
holds at *both* ends.

## B0.1 — the training data has seen crossings

Primary definition, complete instances only (both nodes labelled):
**2,107 of 3,974 eligible frames = 53.02%** have a pair below 100 px. 172 of
4,146 frames are ineligible. Sensitivities, which answer different questions:
all-visible-centroid 2,603/4,146 (62.78%), minimum visible-node 3,325/4,146
(80.20%).

The roadmap expected ≈0, citing §2.10 — but §2.10 measured the Detectron2 crop
set and the 3D masks, both produced by a filter that gates out close contact. It
never covered the SLEAP labels.

Re-confirmed on the latest labels (`round8.top.train` + **round9b** side, 4,500
frames): 62.51% vs 62.78% on the all-visible sensitivity, unchanged conclusion.

**0 of 4,146 training frames have two instances sharing a node.** The human
annotator always separated them.

**Rejecting "never seen" does not show the label mix is adequate.**

## B0.2 — crossing-candidate workload

Full census of all 65 experiments with retained predictions, **reported by group
(n = 12 groups)**: **14,684 link-free encounters = 367.1 h** of annotation;
30,558 counting all three pairs = 764.0 h.

Coverage gap: the recording tree holds 71 experiments in 14 groups, so **groups B
and C have no retained predictions**, nor do `20260115_N_2_3` or the two `_P2`
continuations. The census is complete over the retained predictions, not the corpus.

Counts are top-view only — depth is collapsed, so they are an upper bound on true
3D encounters — and the mm/px figure is an operational screening proxy, not
calibration. Its spread across the corpus is **19.7%** (0.1781–0.2131), with group
G alone spanning 0.1864–0.2131, which is a second independent reason not to treat
it as physical.

**Consequence for Phase A:** 367 h to annotate every candidate is not a plan.
A.3 must sample, and the rule has to be written before annotation starts. Even the
minimum Gate A.3 cohort of three colour-separated BORIS groups (E, K, O) carries
**87.4 h** of candidates.

## B0.3 — architecture comparison on held-out frames

Frames whose canonical `(leaf video, frame_idx)` key is absent from the matching
round-7 package: **113 top, 113 side**, all from `20251225_K_1_2_FCMC` —
**one group, one day**. Primary metric: both-node instance recall @ 20 px after
per-frame Hungarian matching. Criterion fixed in code before the run.

**Crossing-regime recall, and the switch delta:**

| view | arm | top-down | bottom-up | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top | ALL | 73.33% | **80.00%** | **+6.67** |
| top | CORRECTED | 56.72% | **72.39%** | **+15.67** |
| side | ALL | 77.08% | 77.08% | +0.00 |
| side | CORRECTED | 61.47% | 64.22% | +2.75 |

**The top-view result is robust to the contamination**: bottom-up wins by +6.67
on the arm that favours top-down and by +15.67 on the arm that favours bottom-up.
Both clear the frozen +5.0 switch margin. The side view does not, at either end.

**Secondary metrics, both views, both arms — bottom-up dominates:**

- **Shared-node predictions ("one tail joined to two mouths"): top-down 5 (top)
  and 3 (side); bottom-up 0 and 0.** The reported failure is architecture-specific.
- Precision at crossings: top-down 72.69% / 72.91%; bottom-up 92.31% / 91.36%.
  Top-down over-predicts — 227 instances for 225 ground-truth ones while
  recalling 73%.

### Decision under the frozen rule

Rule 1 requires ≥ +5.0 points in **both** views. Top qualifies; side does not.
Rule 2 therefore applies: **views disagree → retain the current architecture and
report per view. Do not average the views.**

Rule 3 also fires: every model falls below the 80% crossing-recall floor in at
least one arm, so **architecture-independent decoder/retraining work is flagged**.

**I am not relaxing the rule after seeing the result.** Bottom-up's dominance on
precision and shared-node rate is real and pre-specified as a *secondary*, and the
switch rule keys on crossing recall. The honest statement is: the frozen rule says
retain top-down, and the rule may well be the wrong rule. Revise it **before** the
next run, not after this one.

## B0.4 — geometric plausibility filter

Two rules, both derived from human labels, never from predictions or from mm:

1. drop an instance sharing a node (< 1 px) with another — human labels never do
   this (B0.1: 0 of 4,146 frames);
2. drop an instance whose mouth–tail span is outside the 0.5–99.5 percentile of
   the labelled distribution: **[35.1, 152.4] px** (top, n=3,903) and
   **[15.9, 142.4] px** (side, n=7,350).

Bounds are deliberately in **pixels**. B0.2's mm/px is an operational proxy, and
the labelled span is shorter than the animal because the `tail` node sits at the
tail base — converting would import an unvalidated assumption into a production
filter.

Effect at crossings (frozen rule: keep if precision rises and recall falls by
≤ 1.0 point):

| view | arch | arm | recall | precision | shared nodes | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top | top-down | ALL | 73.33 → 72.89 (−0.44) | 72.69 → 74.89 (**+2.20**) | **5 → 0** | KEEP |
| top | top-down | CORRECTED | 56.72 → 55.97 (−0.75) | 33.48 → 34.25 (+0.77) | **5 → 0** | KEEP |
| top | bottom-up | ALL | 80.00 → 79.56 (−0.44) | 92.31 → 92.27 (−0.04) | 0 → 0 | reject |
| top | bottom-up | CORRECTED | 72.39 → 72.39 (0.00) | 49.74 → 50.00 (+0.26) | 0 → 0 | KEEP |
| side | top-down | ALL | 77.08 → 76.04 (−1.04) | 72.91 → 73.74 (+0.83) | **3 → 0** | reject |
| side | top-down | CORRECTED | 61.47 → 61.47 (0.00) | 33.00 → 33.84 (+0.83) | **3 → 0** | KEEP |
| side | bottom-up | ALL | 77.08 → 77.08 (0.00) | 91.36 → 93.08 (**+1.72**) | 0 → 0 | KEEP |
| side | bottom-up | CORRECTED | 64.22 → 64.22 (0.00) | 43.21 → 44.03 (+0.82) | 0 → 0 | KEEP |

**Adopt for top-down.** It removes the reported failure mode completely — every
shared-node prediction, in both views, in every arm — for 0 to 1 point of recall.
The two "reject" cells are marginal (−0.04 precision; −1.04 recall against a
−1.00 tolerance) and both concern cases where the filter has little to do:
bottom-up produces no shared nodes to remove.

## B0.5 — not supported, and separately blocked

B0.5 is "retrain with crossing frames". Two independent reasons not to:

1. **The premise is false.** B0.1 shows the training set is already 53% crossing
   frames under the primary definition. It is not short of them.
2. **The failure is not a data-coverage failure.** Shared-node predictions appear
   in top-down and never in bottom-up on the same frames, and never in the human
   labels. That is a decoder/grouping behaviour, not a missing-examples problem.

It is also blocked on annotation that does not exist. **Recommendation: drop B0.5
as written.** If a retrain happens, the available and unexploited asset is
**round9b** (3,118 side frames, every frame with all three instances) which has
**no trained model anywhere on `Z:`** — but expect it to help less than its size
suggests, for the reasons above.

## Gate B0 — met, with the scope stated

The gate asked for a detection-quality number at crossings versus overall, for
both architectures, to decide retrain-vs-switch. That number now exists on frames
held out from both checkpoints, under a criterion fixed before the run, with the
label contamination measured and bracketed.

- **Switch architecture: no**, under the frozen rule — the views disagree.
- **Retrain on crossing frames: no** — the premise is false.
- **Adopt the B0.4 filter for top-down: yes.**
- **Flagged**: architecture-independent decoder work, because every model is below
  the 80% crossing-recall floor somewhere.

## What this does not establish

- **`n_groups = 1`.** Every held-out frame is group K, day 2. This is descriptive
  for one trio. It is not evidence about the other 13 groups.
- **No unbiased evaluation set exists** in the current labels for round-7
  top-down. Closing that needs labels produced independently of both checkpoints —
  which is what Phase A's frozen benchmark is for, and is the strongest argument
  for building it before any further method work.
- **The switch rule may be wrong.** It keys on crossing recall alone, and
  bottom-up wins decisively on precision and shared-node rate in both views. That
  is an argument for revising the rule, in advance, not for overriding it now.
- **Round 8 itself is still unmeasured at crossings.** It is top-down only, and
  its saved validation splits hold 5 crossing frames (top) and 6 (side).
