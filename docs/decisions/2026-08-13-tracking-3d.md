# Tracking the K corpus: 3D, not 2D — and two metrics that could not tell the methods apart

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Tools:** [`c01`](../../tools/c01_s16_predictions.py) (read the s16 chunks once),
[`c02`](../../tools/c02_tracking_probe.py) (2D vs 3D),
[`c03`](../../tools/c03_track_3d.py) (reconstruct + track),
[`c04`](../../tools/c04_score_tracking.py) (score + decision rule)
**New library code:** `medaka3d.calib`, `medaka3d.reconstruct`, `medaka3d.track`,
`medaka3d.eval.tracking`, pinned by `tests/test_calib.py`,
`tests/test_reconstruct.py`, `tests/test_tracking_metrics.py`
**Builds on:** [2026-08-13 reviewed labels](2026-08-13-reviewed-labels-bottomup-3node.md)
**Results:** `work_dir/c02_tracking_probe/`, `work_dir/c03_track_3d/`,
`work_dir/c04_score_tracking/`, each with a provenance sidecar

## Verdict

**Track in 3D.** Solve the cross-view correspondence per frame on the raw
detections, triangulate, then link three points in the tank. The 2D route was
not built.

**Adopt `gated`** — Hungarian on 3D distance, with a derived distance ceiling
and gap bridging. It beats `predictive` under the pre-registered rule, and also
on fragmentation and on encounter preservation, in **all three experiments
separately**; on track jumps the two are statistically indistinguishable
(p = 0.25 / 0.57 / 0.67), so nothing there argues against it. It ties `nearest`
on the rule's primary metric, but that tie is on a metric that cannot separate
them, and on the one test that is not constructional `nearest` is far worse:
**2,649 physically impossible track jumps against `gated`'s 111** — a factor of
15–104 by view, on essentially identical denominators, at p ≤ 5e-159.

**No switch-versus-fragmentation trade-off appeared.** The rule anticipated one
and there is none: `gated` has both the lower switch-candidate count and the
lower fragmentation than `predictive`, and also the higher encounter
preservation. The trade-off that *does* exist is `nearest`'s — perfect
fragmentation and perfect encounter preservation bought by an algorithm that
cannot decline, and paid for in impossible motion.

**Two of the four metrics turned out to be unable to discriminate, and both are
reported as such rather than as results.** One is the pre-registered 2D-vs-3D
rule, which floored at exactly 0.000% on both arms. The other is the
switch-candidate rate, which compares a tracker against the per-frame geometric
assignment — and all three methods are built on the per-frame geometric
assignment, so it can only ever flag the one that deviates deliberately. This is
the same failure `s10` documented for shared-node across architectures, arriving
twice more.

## n_groups = 1, stated before the numbers

Every frame here is **group K**. `K_1_1`, `K_1_2`, `K_1_3` are the same trio of
fish on three consecutive days — repeated measures, not three replicates. The
corpus holds **14 groups** and **13 are unmeasured** by anything below.

What that permits and forbids:

- **Permitted.** Statements about mechanism — that a stale track outbidding a
  fresh one churns identity, that a missing mouth in one camera need not cost
  the frame, that the cross-view correspondence beats its runner-up by an order
  of magnitude. These follow from geometry and arithmetic, not from sampling.
- **Forbidden.** Every absolute rate as a corpus estimate. The three days differ
  from each other already (resolved 97.1 / 96.0 / 95.9%, encounters 361 / 411 /
  455), and those are the *same three fish*. A different trio may sit anywhere.
- **Forbidden.** Anything about which animal is which. Slots and track IDs are
  arbitrary integers. No identity claim is made anywhere in this work.

## Why 3D — and why the rule that was supposed to decide it did not

`c02` measured the two correspondence problems against each other on 15,000
frames (5 contiguous blocks × 1,000 × 3 experiments, `s10`'s block geometry).

### The 2D-vs-3D rule, printed unaltered

> Adopt 3D iff (3D temporal ambiguity < best 2D view) **and** (3D cross-view
> ambiguity < best 2D view), where ambiguous means the runner-up costs less than
> one median winning cost more than the winner.

| | 2D top | 2D side1 | 2D side2 | 3D temporal | 3D cross-view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median winner | 4.17 px | 4.40 px | 4.70 px | 1.10 mm | 1.27 mm |
| median runner-up | 245.3 | 299.7 | 274.6 | 79.0 | 15.4 |
| **ambiguous** | **0.000%** | **0.000%** | **0.000%** | **0.000%** | **0.271%** |
| verdict | | | | FAIL | FAIL |
| **the rule says** | | | | | **track in 2D** |

**The rule is degenerate here and its answer means nothing.** With a median
winner of 4.2 px and a median runner-up of 245 px, "the runner-up is within one
typical winning cost" can essentially never happen, so the statistic measured its
own denominator. Both temporal arms floored at exactly 0, and the comparison then
turned on which side of a tie `<` falls. The rule is recorded above as written
and applied as written; it did not decide anything.

### What did decide it, chosen after seeing the floor

Three measurements, added to `c02` and labelled post-hoc there and here.

**1. Credible-rival rate** — a runner-up counts only if it costs no more than
the p99.9 of that metric's own winners. One criterion, four metrics, each
yardstick derived from its own data and printed.

| | 2D top | 2D side1 | 2D side2 | 3D temporal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| credible rival | 0.0705% | 0.0973% | 0.0074% | **0.0000%** |
| `s10` half-span gate | 29.56% | 18.75% | 17.57% | **7.96%** |

The 3D temporal step is cleaner than the best 2D view under both criteria — and
under `s10`'s own gate it is **2.2× to 3.7×** cleaner. (That gate is generous by
construction: half a body length in 6 ms is ~2 m/s, which no medaka does. Its
value is that it is the project's existing threshold, applied unchanged to both
arms, so the *ratio* is meaningful even though the levels are not.)

**2. Coverage, which is the real difference.** A single camera reports exactly
three detections in 94.2–96.6% of frames. **At least two cameras do in 98.96%**,
and triangulation needs two, not three. Of the frames where a view loses an
animal, **76–90% have both other views intact** — recoverable in 3D, and in 2D
a track break that must be re-acquired across a gap.

| view | frames it lost an animal | of those, both other views intact |
|---|---|---|
| top | 505 | 76.2% |
| side1 | 874 | 89.7% |
| side2 | 839 | 85.9% |

**3. The cross-view correspondence is not a cost 3D pays and 2D avoids.** This
is the argument that settles it, and it is structural rather than measured. The
project's output must be 3D — encounters are defined in mm, the research question
is about 3D behaviour — so *both* routes have to solve cross-view correspondence.
3D solves it per frame on raw detections, where the winner beats the runner-up
**12×** (1.27 mm against 15.38 mm) and is ambiguous in 0.271% of frames. The 2D
route defers it until three independent view-trackers have already broken in
three different places, and then solves the same problem with strictly less
information.

Given that the temporal step is not the bottleneck in either representation,
"which route does the cross-view correspondence better" *is* the decision, and
per-frame-on-detections wins it.

### What choosing 3D costs, stated plainly

Frames where fewer than two views report three usable detections cannot be
reconstructed at all: **2.9 / 4.1 / 4.2%** of the three days. A 2D tracker in the
top camera would still have had something to link in most of those. The 3D route
declines them; that is a deliberate refusal, and the fallback that would rescue
them (a two-view solve with a partial instance, or linking through the pectoral)
is not built.

## The correctness fix that recovered 9 points of coverage

The first full run resolved only 86.5–88.7% of frames, against the 98.96%
`c02` predicted from block sampling. The gap was not sampling.

**One missing mouth in one camera was costing the whole frame, in all three
cameras.** The bottom-up model leaves a mouth unplaced in 2.9–4.2% of otherwise
complete frames per view; a NaN pixel gives a NaN ray, and since every one of the
36 correspondence hypotheses contains every detection, all 36 go non-finite
together. `c03` now requires finite mouths before a view counts as complete, so
the offending camera is dropped and the other two carry the frame.

| | resolved before | resolved after |
|---|---|---|
| K_1_1 | 88.65% | **97.13%** |
| K_1_2 | 87.97% | **95.95%** |
| K_1_3 | 86.45% | **95.85%** |

Triangulation failures went from 33,324–39,629 per experiment to **zero**.

**The pectoral is never missing.** Across all three views and all three days,
0.000% of complete frames lack a pectoral, against 2.9–4.2% for the mouth and
4.3–4.8% for the tail. It is the middle node of the chain and the PAF hub, and on
this evidence it is the better tracking anchor. Nothing here was changed to use
it — the mouth is what `s10`'s permutation metric links on and switching anchors
mid-comparison would have made the comparison meaningless — but it is the
cheapest available improvement and is listed under Next.

## Reconstruction quality

| experiment | ≥2 views | resolved | 3 views | 2 views | residual median | correspondence margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K_1_1 | 97.13% | 97.13% | 76.41% | 20.72% | 0.380 mm | 13.86 mm |
| K_1_2 | 95.95% | 95.95% | 75.37% | 20.58% | 0.387 mm | 12.44 mm |
| K_1_3 | 95.85% | 95.85% | 73.53% | 22.32% | 0.433 mm | 11.96 mm |

The residual is the point-to-ray RMS of the winning correspondence. **0.38–0.43
mm on live fish against the calibration's own 0.24 mm rms on held-out wet
checkerboards** — the reconstruction is performing at the calibration's accuracy,
not below it. The margin column is the runner-up correspondence's cost minus the
winner's: ~12–14 mm, half a body length, which is why the correspondence is
solvable at all.

The refractive raycaster was checked against the calibration's own evidence
before any of this: triangulating its 736 ball-marker poses reproduces a
point-to-ray median of 0.25 mm (the manifest claims 0.22 mm over a larger,
easier set), recovers the rigid rig's known 25–86.6 mm inter-marker distances to
0.77 mm rms over 3,697 pairs, and places the reconstructed volume at
x ∈ [−2.4, 190.4], y ∈ [−0.3, 192.2], z ∈ [−4.9, 181.1] mm — the tank is 192 ×
192 × 186 mm and the code was never told so.

## The three methods

Each differs from the last by exactly one mechanism, so a difference in score has
one candidate explanation. They are three functions, not one function with a
flag (design rule 5).

- **`nearest`** — Hungarian on 3D distance every frame. No gate, no memory. It
  cannot decline.
- **`gated`** — plus a distance ceiling and gap bridging. It can decline.
- **`predictive`** — plus constant-velocity prediction, matching against where a
  track was *going*. The hypothesis is specific: two animals passing close are
  ambiguous by position and separable by direction.

Thresholds, all derived from this data before any tracker ran, and printed:

| | value | derivation |
|---|---|---|
| `max_step_mm` | 10.2 mm | p99.9 of per-animal 3D mouth displacement under the forced assignment (median 0.359, p99 1.875, max 204.5) |
| `max_gap_frames` | 60 (361 ms) | p99 of the observed unresolved-run lengths (n = 5,365, median 2, max 3,474) |
| `velocity_frames` | 3 (18 ms) | **a choice, not a derivation** |
| 3D gate (metrics) | 12.40 mm | half the median reconstructed body length, 24.80 mm over 2,256,000 instances |
| 2D gates (metrics) | 60 / 50 / 50 px | `s10`'s half-median-span from `labels.round8.<view>.train` |

## A defect in my own tracker, found by arithmetic that did not add up

The first scored run had `gated` producing **864 tracks** while **53% of adjacent
frame pairs carried a different set of three track IDs**. Those two numbers
cannot both describe healthy tracking, and the second is not visible in any
headline: the track *count* was low and the switch rate read 0.000%.

The cause: the link ceiling widens with how long a track has been missing, so
gap bridging works. It was being folded into the **cost** — dividing distance by
the ceiling before solving — which inverts the comparison. A 60-frame-old track
30 mm away scored 30/(6×60) = 0.083 against a 1-frame-old track 1 mm away at
1/6 = 0.167, so **stale tracks systematically stole slots from fresh ones** and
the identity set churned every few frames.

Fixed: the assignment is made on raw distance, the ceiling only rejects.
`tests/test_reconstruct.py::test_a_stale_track_does_not_outbid_a_fresh_one` pins
it, and the companion test pins that bridging still works. Every `gated` and
`predictive` number in this document is from after the fix.

The fix moved every metric it could touch, which is why the pre-fix run is not
reported anywhere as a result:

| `gated` | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| transitions excluded from the switch metric | 534,928 | **1,707** |
| encounter preservation | 29.58% | **82.56%** |
| tracks | 864 | 1,455 |

`nearest` is unaffected — it has no ceiling, so there was nothing to fold.

**Neither of the two headline metrics caught this.** The switch rate read a
perfect 0.0000% throughout, and the track count read *better* before the fix than
after. What caught it was the exclusion counter — the field that records what a
metric declined to score. That field exists because a rate without its
denominator is not a measurement, and it is the reason this defect is in this
document instead of in the conclusions.

### The switch-candidate rate cannot separate these three, and here is why

It asks whether a tracker's frame-to-frame correspondence disagrees with the
minimum-displacement assignment. **All three methods are built on the
minimum-displacement assignment**, so:

- `nearest` *is* that assignment. It cannot score anything but 0. Its
  0/1,000,936 is a definition, not a measurement.
- `gated` differs only by refusing links and by bridging gaps, and a refusal
  removes the transition from scoring rather than producing a disagreement. Its
  0/1,000,170 is very nearly a definition too.
- `predictive` is the only one that can genuinely deviate, because it matches
  against predicted positions. Its 29 disagreements are the metric working
  exactly once.

So a primary metric that reads 0, 0, 29 is not ranking three trackers; it is
counting how far each departs from the geometry it was built on. **This is the
same failure `s10` recorded for shared-node across architectures** — a metric
measuring its own construction — and it is why the rule's tie is not evidence
that `gated` and `nearest` are equally good.

### What actually separates them

**Track jumps** are the one test that compares a tracker against physics rather
than against another algorithm: a single track whose mouth moved more than half
a body length in 6 ms. Nothing is constructional about it.

| comparison | top | side1 | side2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gated` vs `nearest` | 0.0003% vs 0.0311%, p = 5e-187 | 0.0021% vs 0.0323%, p = 5e-159 | 0.0016% vs 0.0325%, p = 2e-168 |
| `gated` vs `predictive` | 0.0003% vs 0.0001%, p = 0.25 | 0.0021% vs 0.0019%, p = 0.57 | 0.0016% vs 0.0015%, p = 0.67 |

`nearest` asserts **2,649 impossible motions**; `gated` asserts 111 and
`predictive` 97, and those two are indistinguishable from each other on all
three views. `nearest`'s perfect track count and perfect encounter preservation
are what an algorithm that cannot decline looks like from the inside — it never
breaks a track because it never refuses one, and it pays in physics.

**Encounter preservation** then separates `gated` from `predictive`:
**82.56% vs 69.03%, p = 5.1e-15**, consistent in all three days (83.1 / 80.0 /
84.4 against 74.5 / 68.9 / 64.8). Combined with fewer tracks and zero switch
candidates, `gated` beats `predictive` outright, with no trade to report.

**The constant-velocity model was a specific hypothesis and it lost.** It was
built on the argument that two animals passing close are ambiguous by position
and separable by direction. Against a 166 fps camera and a 0.36 mm median
per-frame step, prediction adds noise: a 3-frame velocity estimate over steps
that small is dominated by the detector's own ~0.4 mm localisation error, so it
displaces the match target without informing it. It fragments more, preserves
fewer encounters, and produces the only geometry-contradicting links in the set.

### Three-view consistency

Re-derived from a node the trackers do not link on, so it is not circular:

| pairing | agreement | n |
|---|---|---|
| pectoral: top ~ side1 | **96.97%** | 863,812 |
| pectoral: top ~ side2 | **97.47%** | 868,300 |
| tail: top ~ side1 | 96.70% | 785,287 |
| tail: top ~ side2 | 96.81% | 790,976 |

**This scores the reconstruction, not the tracker** — it is identical for all
three methods, because they share one per-frame correspondence. Read it as an
upper bound on cross-view correspondence error: ~2.5–3.0% of frames, and part of
that is the checking node being wrong rather than the correspondence.

**The first version of this number was 90.6% and it was misleading.** It was
computed on the tail without excluding frames where a tail was missing, and a
NaN point makes some pairing cheapest for no geometric reason. Restricting to
frames where the tail is actually present moves it to 96.7–96.8%. The pectoral —
never missing — gives 97.0–97.5% and is what is reported.

## What could and could not be measured without ground truth

**There is no human track for M1 or M2 anywhere in this project**, and none was
made this session. Ground truth labels the female alone.

**Measured, and load-bearing:**

- Geometric impossibility of a tracker's own output (track jumps). This is what
  chose the method.
- Fragmentation, coverage, track-length distributions.
- Reconstruction accuracy against the calibration's own validated figure
  (0.38–0.43 mm on fish against 0.24 mm rms on held-out wet checkerboards).
- Cross-view agreement against an independent node.
- Whether a correspondence was forced (winner-to-runner-up margins).

**Not measurable, and not claimed:**

- **Whether a preserved identity is the right identity.** `nearest` scores 100%
  encounter preservation and is the worst tracker in the set. Preservation
  without a swap check rewards confidence, and every number in the "enc kept"
  column inherits that.
- **The swap rate.** A tracker that exchanges two animals mid-contact and keeps
  both IDs continuous is invisible to every metric here.
- **Whether `predictive`'s 29 disagreements are errors or corrections.** They are
  the only cases where any method departed from per-frame geometry. Without a
  human they cannot be adjudicated — which is why they are called *candidates*.
- **Whether the ~3% cross-view disagreement is correspondence error or pectoral
  error.**
- **Naming.** Nothing here says which track is the female.

### What an M1/M2 ground truth would cost

The unit is the **encounter**, not the frame — `medaka3d.eval.metrics` says why:
at 166 fps a frame-wise identity accuracy is dominated by the long separated
stretches and reads >99% while swapping at every crossing. The reconstruction
now makes the encounter set computable, and for these three K days it is:

| | K_1_1 | K_1_2 | K_1_3 | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| encounters | 361 | 411 | 455 | **1,227** |
| median duration | 2.3 s | 2.5 s | 2.6 s | 2.4 s |
| ≥ 1 s | 287 | 338 | 368 | 993 |
| clip video to watch | | | | 114 min |

The annotator marks three animals at **entry** and again at **exit**, preserving
correspondence — `EncounterTruth`, which is method-agnostic and so is annotated
once and reused by every future method. That is **2 frames and 6 instance marks
per encounter**, plus watching a clip that begins and ends in separation.

| goal | encounters | marked frames | instance marks | video | annotation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| first signal — tell 80% survival from 50% | **120** (40 × 3 groups) | 240 | 720 | ~11 min | ~3 h |
| powered — ±5% on a rate, 80% power for a 10-point difference | **390** (130 × 3 groups) | 780 | 2,340 | ~36 min | ~10 h |

Annotation hours use `summarise`'s 1.5 min/encounter estimate, which is itself a
prior and not a measurement. **The groups matter more than the count**: 390
encounters from group K alone would still be n_groups = 1 and would answer
nothing the numbers above do not. ROADMAP A.3 already requires ≥ 3 groups, and
that requirement is the binding one.

## Against the ROADMAP

**ROADMAP says "Phase C is forbidden until Gate A exists"**, and Gate A — a
human-annotated encounter benchmark — does not exist. This session ran Phase C
work anyway, at the experimenter's explicit direction and with prediction-only
metrics. The consequence is visible above and is exactly what the gate was
written to prevent: the primary metric could not separate the methods, and the
method was chosen on a secondary one. The gate is not wrong; this ran ahead of
it knowingly.

## Next

1. **Build the encounter ground truth.** 390 encounters across ≥ 3 groups, per
   the table above. It is the one thing that would make any of these numbers a
   statement about identity rather than about geometry, and it is annotated once
   for every future method.
2. **Switch the tracking anchor to the pectoral.** It is missing in 0.000% of
   complete frames where the mouth is missing in 2.9–4.2% and the tail in
   4.3–4.8%. That is the cheapest coverage gain available and it needs no
   retraining. Keep the mouth for the `s10`-lineage metrics so the comparison
   history stays valid.
3. **Stitch fragments (ROADMAP C.4).** The real problem is **~99–178 substantial
   fragments across the three days**, not 1,455 — the rest is debris of ≤10
   frames. A break is recoverable and a swap is not, which is the right way
   round, but the breaks have to be repaired before any behavioural quantity can
   be computed, and at this size an explicit path-cover over ~60 fragments per
   experiment is tractable rather than aspirational.
4. **Reconstruct the 2.9–4.2% of frames that fewer than two views can carry**, or
   state them as a permanent hole. A two-view solve using partial instances is
   the obvious route.
5. **Extend beyond group K.** Every number here is n_groups = 1. The corpus has
   14 groups; the detector already exists for all of them.
6. **Register in `MANIFEST.json`**: the promoted calibration bundle, the s16
   predictions, the c03 tracks, and the still-unregistered locked HITL-v2
   benchmark.
